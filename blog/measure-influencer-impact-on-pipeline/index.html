How to Measure Which Deals Your Influencers, Analysts and Partners Actually Touched

Influence is hard to track, so it gets cut first. Here's a simple way to measure which deals analysts, influencers and partners touched.

1 Nov 2026By Colin DowlingPart of The BoFu Map

Key takeaways

  • If you can't show influence touched pipeline, it gets cut first.
  • Ask every new lead how they heard about you, in their own words.
  • Tag deals where an analyst, influencer or partner came up, and compare outcomes.
  • Directional evidence is enough; perfect attribution isn't coming.

To measure which deals an analyst, influencer or partner touched, ask every new lead how they heard about you in their own words, have sales tag deals where those names come up, and compare how those deals perform. It won't be perfect attribution, but it's honest evidence that holds up in a budget meeting.

A consultant I talked with recently, who has spent years helping companies work with the experts buyers trust, said the first step in proving impact on pipeline is measurement. You need some way to see where incoming leads are coming from.

It sounds obvious. Almost nobody does it well.

Why influence gets cut first

When budgets tighten, the first things cut are the ones nobody can measure. Influence is a classic example. An analyst mentions you. A respected voice in your market recommends you on a podcast. A partner tells a client to call you. None of that leaves a clean click.

So the dashboard gives credit to the last thing the buyer clicked, usually a branded search or a direct visit. The influence that actually created the interest disappears.

Here's the tension. On one hand, you know influence matters. You've seen buyers mention it on calls. On the other hand, "trust me" doesn't hold up in a budget review.

Three simple ways to see it

You don't need expensive software. You need a few habits.

Ask buyers how they heard about you

Add a free-text field to your demo or contact form: "How did you hear about us?" Not a dropdown. Let them write it. You'll see names of analysts, podcasts, people and communities that no tracking tool would ever catch.

Then ask again on the first sales call. Buyers often give a fuller answer when talking.

Have sales tag deals

Ask reps to note in the CRM when an analyst report, an influencer, a partner or a community came up during a deal. One field, a short list of options, plus "other." Make it part of the stage change, so it actually gets filled in.

Compare outcomes

After a quarter or two, compare deals where influence came up with deals where it didn't:

  • Win rate.
  • Deal size.
  • Time to close.

If influenced deals close faster or at higher rates, you have a story that holds up.

Don't wait for perfect

Perfect attribution isn't coming. Buyers research in private conversations, through AI tools and in places you'll never see. The goal is directional evidence, good enough to make decisions.

A spreadsheet that shows "these 12 deals mentioned this analyst, and they closed 30 percent faster" will do more for your budget than any attribution model.

Make influence easier to find

Measurement tells you influence works. The next step is making sure buyers who hear about you can find something useful when they look. That usually means pages that answer the questions they're asking right before they decide: comparisons, alternatives, pricing and fit.

Influence starts the conversation. Bottom-of-funnel pages finish it.

Signs influence is invisible in your data

  • "Direct" and "branded search" are your biggest lead sources, and nobody knows why.
  • Buyers mention podcasts, analysts or peers on calls, but the CRM has no field for it.
  • Influence programs get cut every time budgets tighten.

What to do this week

Add one free-text field, "How did you hear about us?", to your demo form today. Add one CRM field for "influence mentioned" with a short list of options. Tell reps to fill it at the first-meeting stage. In a month you'll have the start of a dataset nobody in your market is tracking, and in a quarter you'll have something to compare.

A worked example

Say your demo form starts asking "How did you hear about us?" After a quarter, a pattern shows up: a meaningful share of answers mention the same industry newsletter, while your attribution tool credited those leads to "direct." Now compare those deals with the rest on win rate and time to close. If they're better, you have a case to invest more in that relationship. If they're not, you've learned that too, and you spent nothing to find out.

Common mistakes

  • Using a dropdown instead of free text. You'll only learn about the sources you already knew.
  • Waiting for perfect attribution. It isn't coming, so start with directional evidence.

Questions

How do you measure the impact of analysts or influencers on sales?

Combine a few simple methods. Ask every new lead how they heard about you in a free-text field, ask sales to note when an analyst, influencer or partner comes up in deals, and compare win rates and deal speed for those deals versus others. It won't be perfect attribution, but it's honest evidence.

What is self-reported attribution?

Self-reported attribution means asking buyers directly how they found you, usually with an open text field on a form or a question on the first sales call. It captures sources tracking software misses, like podcasts, word of mouth, analyst reports and AI tool recommendations. Many B2B teams find it more revealing than click data.

Why is B2B attribution so hard?

Buyers research across many channels, talk to colleagues privately, read analyst reports, ask AI tools and visit your site only near the end. Most of that leaves no trackable click. Software attribution gives credit to the last visible touch, which often undervalues the influence that created the interest in the first place.

Is influencer marketing worth it for B2B?

It can be, when the influencers are trusted by your actual buyers and you can show some connection to pipeline. Start small, track self-reported sources and deal notes, and compare results with other channels after a few months. Keep what shows up in real deals.
