Earn an Audience Through the Research Community Before You Pitch

Walking into purchasing with a pitch goes nowhere in a cautious market. Earn a hearing first through the research side and written work people already read.

4 Nov 2026By Colin DowlingPart of how AI citation is measured

Key takeaways

  • Purchasing is the last door, not the first, in a market that hates being early.
  • An audience is something you earn on the research side, in writing, before you ask for a meeting.
  • White papers and practitioner writeups are proof content, not a brochure with footnotes.
  • Answer engines cite that written work. A homepage pitch gives them nothing to repeat.

You can have a product a cautious buyer needs and still get nowhere, if the first conversation you ask for is the purchase. In markets where nobody wants to be early, you have to be known before you are sold.

A healthcare imaging executive I interviewed on the Modern Business Life podcast said it without dressing it up. You have to get an audience. You cannot walk into the purchasing department and announce that you have something they should buy. He has watched that path go nowhere. You have to work the research side of the business, so people understand what you do in written white papers.

He was talking about hospitals and imaging, where buyers do not like risk and a familiar name feels safer. In that world, a name like Epic carries weight because a lot of people are already in the pool. A company without that kind of recognition does not fix it with a sharper feature list at the purchasing desk. It earns a hearing first, among the people who read, write, and teach.

The same pattern shows up anywhere a bad choice is a career risk, not just a budget one. The committee is looking for something their own experts have already seen in writing.

Get the audience before you ask for the meeting

An audience, in his sense, is not a follower count. It is a group of practitioners who could explain what you do to a colleague without you in the room. If they cannot, you do not have an audience yet. You have a list.

That is a distribution problem, and it is slower than a campaign. I have made the same point about building distribution before you launch. Attention you did not earn in advance has to be rented, and rented attention does not survive a cautious buying group.

The research community still gives attention when you show the work: studies, methods, white papers, a talk that teaches the problem before it mentions the product. If the paper reads like a demo script, that audience will treat it like one. A deal that started in a paper or a talk will not show up as a click, either. Measuring which deals those voices actually touched is how you keep funding the slow work when someone asks why you are not just emailing purchasing.

Write the paper so a stranger can restate it

A white paper earns its place when a reader who has never met you can say, afterward, what you do and what you are not claiming. That is a higher bar than "it looks serious."

A few tests I use:

  • The first page names the problem in the reader's language. Not your category. The situation they already have.
  • The method is visible. What was examined, over what kind of work, with what limit. A paper that skips the method is a brochure.
  • The product shows up late, and narrowly. Where it fits the finding. Not a feature tour glued to the end.
  • A skeptical peer could forward it. If they would be embarrassed to pass it on, it is not research content. It is a pitch with a longer PDF.

Publish it where that community already looks, and publish a plain HTML version, not only a gated file. Answer engines and busy researchers both have to read the claim without booking a meeting. Then put the same claims, in the same words, on the pages a buyer will check next. Inconsistent descriptions are how answer engines get you wrong. A paper that says one thing and a homepage that says another makes you look muddled.

Connect it to the page that catches the search

Research content creates the audience. Bottom-of-funnel content catches the person who is now willing to look you up.

When someone from that community finally searches, or asks an AI tool who does this kind of work, they use specific language. The finding. The workflow. The comparison with the thing they already use. Your site needs a page that answers in kind: what you do, what the written work showed, what it did not show, and where you fit. How AI citation is measured is the habit of checking whether those answers name you or name a safer incumbent.

The paper and the decision page are a pair. The paper is why a cautious reader takes you seriously. The comparison page is why that turns into a shortlist. Pull three sentences a committee could use unchanged, put them on that page, and ask a practitioner who knows the work if they are fair. If they flinch, fix the sentences before you send anyone there. A PDF your own experts will not stand next to is not an audience.

What to do this week

List the last few serious pitches your team aimed at a buying office. Next to each, note whether anyone on that account had already read a non-sales explanation of what you do. The blanks are the point.

Then draft the answer to one question those readers already argue about: the problem, the evidence you have, the evidence you do not, and one narrow note on where the product fits. Have someone outside marketing tell you what they think you do. If they cannot, it is not ready.

A worked example

A smaller imaging vendor wants a meeting with a hospital system. The old motion is a note to purchasing and a deck about platform, AI, and a modern architecture. Nothing in that note gives a radiologist a reason to sponsor the risk.

The other motion starts earlier, on the research side. A written paper on a specific reading workflow, with the method and the limit, discussed where those readers already gather. The site states the same finding in sentences a committee can forward, and a comparison says who this is for and who should stay with the incumbent. When a champion walks it into purchasing, the vendor is already a name in a paper the clinical lead has seen. The meeting is the second step.

Common mistakes

  • Leading with purchasing. In a cautious market that meeting is the result of an audience, not the way you get one.
  • Gating the only good explanation. If the clearest account of your work sits behind a form, researchers and answer engines will cite someone else.
  • A paper that is a brochure. Length is not seriousness. A visible method and a visible limit are.
  • No decision page behind the paper. Interest from the research side dies if the site cannot answer a buying question in the same voice.

Questions

Why doesn't a direct pitch to purchasing work in cautious B2B markets?

In risk-averse markets, the buying group does not want to be first, and purchasing is set up to reduce risk, not to discover you. A cold pitch asks them to sponsor a vendor nobody inside has heard of. You have to be known, in writing, by the people those buyers already read, before that meeting is worth having.

What does it mean to work the research side of the business?

It means showing up where practitioners and researchers already pay attention: studies, white papers, talks, and technical writeups that explain what you do and what the evidence is. The goal is understanding, not a close. When those readers later sit on a buying team, you are no longer a stranger.

How is a white paper different from bottom-of-funnel content?

A white paper earns the audience and documents the work. A bottom-of-funnel page catches the buyer who then goes looking: comparisons, fit, implementation, and what the paper does not claim. You need both. The paper without the decision page strands the interest. The decision page without the paper has nothing behind it.

How does research content help you get cited by AI answer engines?

Answer engines assemble an answer from pages that explain a specific question in citable sentences. A clear white paper, and a web page that states the same findings without the pitch, give them a source. A page that only says you are innovative does not get named when a buyer asks what is actually known.
