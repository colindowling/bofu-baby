ChatGPT and other AI tools describe your company wrong when the sources they learn from disagree: your site says one thing, your LinkedIn page another, an old directory listing a third. Pick one clear description of who you help and what you solve, use it everywhere, and publish pages that answer buyer questions directly.

A go-to-market consultant I talked with recently explained the chain reaction well. When messaging isn't consistent across a brand, the LLM gets confused and doesn't know who you are. Then you lose track of yourself in these answer engines.

That's a new problem, and it's growing fast.

## How AI tools build a picture of you

When a buyer asks an AI tool "what does [your company] do?" or "best [category] for [their situation]," the tool draws on what's been published: your website, review sites, directory listings, articles, social profiles, even job postings.

If those all say roughly the same thing, the tool can describe you clearly and confidently. If they don't, it blends them. You end up described as something vague, outdated or simply wrong. Or you don't get mentioned at all, because the tool can't tell what you're the right answer for.

## Where the inconsistency comes from

Most companies drift slowly:

- The homepage was rewritten last year. The about page wasn't.
- The LinkedIn company description is from two pivots ago.
- Each salesperson describes the company differently on their profile.
- Directory and review listings still show an old category or product name.
- Press releases use whatever positioning was current that month.

Each one is small. Together they tell AI tools five different stories.

## Pick one description

Start with a single sentence everyone agrees on: "[Company] helps [specific customer] [solve specific problem]." Then a short paragraph that adds how and why you're different.

Make it specific. "Helps businesses grow" tells an AI tool nothing. "Helps mid-size accounting firms automate client onboarding" tells it exactly when to recommend you.

## Use it everywhere

Then go update the places that matter:

- Homepage, about page and main product pages.
- LinkedIn company page and key team profiles.
- Review sites and directory listings.
- Partner pages that describe you.
- Sales decks and boilerplate for press.

This is boring work. It's also one of the cheapest ways to show up better in AI answers.

## Give AI tools something to cite

Consistency tells AI tools who you are. Specific pages tell them when to recommend you. Buyers ask questions like "best X for Y," "X vs. Y" and "alternatives to X." If you have clear, honest pages answering those questions, AI tools have something to pull from.

Write those pages to answer the question in the first sentence or two, then go deeper. That's useful for human readers too.

## Check it yourself

Once a month, ask the major AI tools the questions your buyers ask. Note whether you're mentioned and how you're described. When something's wrong, trace it back to the source and fix it.

## Signs AI tools are confused about you

- Ask ChatGPT what your company does and the answer is vague or out of date.
- You're missing from "best [category] for [situation]" answers where you should fit.
- AI tools mention a product name or category you stopped using.
- Different tools describe you differently.

## What to do this week

Ask three AI tools the same two questions: "What does [your company] do?" and "What are the best [category] options for [your ideal customer]?" Screenshot the answers. Then compare them with your homepage, your LinkedIn page and your top directory listing. Fix the oldest description first. Re-run the questions next month and see what changed.

## A worked example

Say your homepage calls you "a revenue intelligence platform," LinkedIn says "sales analytics software," and an old directory listing still says "CRM add-on." An AI tool asked for "the best tool to forecast pipeline for mid-size sales teams" has three conflicting descriptions to work with and no clear reason to pick you. Rewrite all three as one sentence: "[Company] helps mid-size sales teams forecast pipeline accurately." Now the tool has a consistent, specific match.

## Common mistakes

- **Fixing only the homepage.** AI tools read directories, reviews and profiles too.
- **Using vague category language.** "Helps businesses grow" gives a tool nothing to match.
- **Checking once.** Answers change, so recheck monthly.
