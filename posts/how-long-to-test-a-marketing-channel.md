How long to test a marketing channel before quitting is about three months, often near 100 days. Set the window and the success measure before you start, watch early indicators so you can adjust, and judge the channel on qualified pipeline at the end rather than on patience halfway through.

A marketing agency founder we talked with recently tells clients, up front, that ads started from scratch need about a hundred days before anyone can see if they will work. Sometimes the results don't show up until day 101.

That lines up with what we see. The more patient a team can be with pipeline, the better a full sales cycle looks. Most channels die because someone ran out of patience, not because the channel failed.

## Why do channels get killed too early?

The pattern is familiar. A team launches ads or starts publishing content. Four weeks in, the numbers are thin. Someone asks if it's working. Nobody can say yes with confidence. So it gets cut, and the team tries something else. Then the same thing happens with the next channel. That cycle is how a plan turns into [random acts of marketing](/blog/random-acts-of-marketing/): activity that never gets a fair window.

The trouble is that most B2B channels have a warm-up period:

- **Paid ads** need data to learn which audiences and messages work.
- **Content and SEO** need months for pages to rank.
- **Outbound** needs a few rounds to find the message that lands.
- **Sales cycles** mean a lead from this month may not close for a quarter or more.

Judging any of these at week four is like judging a garden the day after you plant it.

## How do you set the test before you start?

The fix is simple: decide the rules up front, in writing.

- **The window.** How long you'll run it before deciding. For most channels, about 90 to 100 days.
- **The budget.** What you'll spend in that window.
- **The success measure.** Usually qualified leads or pipeline, with a target.
- **The early signals.** What you'll watch to adjust along the way.
- **The stop rules.** What would make you end it early.

Agree on these with whoever controls the budget. The searches that budget should be aimed at are a finite set, which is what [the BoFu Map](/bofu-map/) counts, so the test is pointed at buyers who are already shopping. Then, when someone asks "is it working?" at week four, there is an answer: the test is on track, or it is being adjusted, and the decision waits until day 100.

## What should you watch before judging pipeline?

You don't have to fly blind for three months. Watch the early signals that tell you whether you're heading the right way:

- Are the right people seeing and clicking?
- Are landing pages converting at a reasonable rate?
- What's the early cost per qualified lead?
- Are sales conversations starting?

Use those to adjust: change the message, the audience, the page. But don't make the final call on them. The final call is about qualified leads and pipeline.

## When should you stop a channel early?

Patience isn't the same as stubbornness. Stop before the window ends if:

- You can't reach the right audience at any reasonable cost.
- Every lead is unqualified, even after real changes.
- The cost per lead is wildly out of line with what a customer is worth.

Those are signs the channel doesn't fit, not that it's slow.

## What has to be in place before a fair test?

A fair test needs somewhere for the traffic to land. If the site and the pages can't convert buyers, every channel will look like it failed. Before you test a channel, make sure there's a page built for the buyer it's sending you.
