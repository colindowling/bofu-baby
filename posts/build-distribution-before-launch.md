The best time to build an audience is months before you launch. Start sharing what you're learning about the problem publicly, make the founder the voice of the company, and pair that presence with pages that catch buyers when they search. Launching to nobody is the hardest way to start.

A founder I talked with recently did something most founders don't. A full year before his product went into beta, he started building distribution, even though he'd never built any kind of social presence before. Looking back on his earlier businesses, he said not having that public presence held him back from really scaling.

I've said it on the pod before: distribution is the only moat that's left.

## Why launching without an audience is so hard

When you launch with no audience, every customer has to be found from scratch. You're paying for every bit of attention. And because nobody knows you, the first people who see you have no reason to trust you.

When you launch with an audience, even a small one, the first customers come from people who've been following along. They know what you're building and why. Some of them have been waiting for it.

That difference shows up in the first year's revenue.

## The founder is the cheapest channel

For most early-stage B2B companies, the founder's public presence is the most effective marketing channel available. It's free, it's credible and it compounds.

Buyers trust people more than logos. A founder who shares useful ideas about a problem becomes someone buyers want to hear from. Partners notice. Good hires notice. Investors notice.

The hard part isn't the cost. It's starting, especially if you've never done it.

## How to start

Keep it simple:

- **Pick one platform** where your buyers spend time. For B2B, often LinkedIn.
- **Share what you're learning.** About the problem, the market, the conversations you're having.
- **Be specific.** One clear idea per post beats a vague thought.
- **Keep a rhythm you can hold.** Two or three times a week, every week.
- **Talk with people, not at them.** Reply to comments. Comment on others' posts.

You don't need to be polished. You need to be useful and consistent.

## Start before the product is ready

You don't need a product to have something to say. In fact, the months before launch are when you have the most interesting things to share: what you're seeing in the market, what buyers told you, what you got wrong.

Sharing the journey builds people who care how it turns out.

## Pair presence with pages

Social builds trust and awareness. But when a buyer is ready to purchase, they often go to Google or ask an AI tool: "[category] alternatives," "best [category] for [situation]," "[your company] pricing."

If there's nothing there to catch them, the attention you built leaks away. So as your presence grows, build pages that answer those buying questions. Your audience starts the conversation. Those pages turn it into pipeline.

## Be patient with it

Building an audience is slow at first. Most founders quit in the first couple of months because the numbers look small. The ones who keep going usually find it becomes one of their best sources of customers.

## Signs you're launching to nobody

- Your launch plan is a press release, a Product Hunt post and hope.
- Fewer than a few hundred people in your market know your name.
- The founder has never posted publicly about the problem the product solves.

## What to do this week

Write down ten things you've learned about your customers' problem in the last year. Each one is a post. Schedule the first three for the next two weeks on the platform your buyers use. Then list the five questions buyers ask right before they choose a vendor in your category, and check whether your site answers any of them. That's the start of both halves: presence and pages.

## What a simple rhythm looks like

Two posts a week is enough to start. One shares something you learned from a buyer conversation. The other shares an opinion about how your market works, with a reason. Reply to every comment in the first hour. Once a month, turn the post that got the most thoughtful responses into a longer page on your site. That's how social presence and search presence start feeding each other.
