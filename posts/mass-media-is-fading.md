Mass media is fading for B2B, and it isn't coming back. The places where a big audience once gathered in one spot keep losing it. The marketing that works now shows up where a specific buyer is actively looking, especially in search results and AI answers, right before they choose.

A marketing leader I talked with recently said it plainly: the idea of mass media has already gone away. The big news organizations will keep losing viewers. Attention has scattered, and it isn't regrouping.

It's been my experience that most marketing budgets haven't caught up with that.

## What changed

There used to be a few places where everyone looked. Buy a slot there and you reached your market.

That world is gone. Attention now lives in thousands of smaller places: niche newsletters, podcasts, communities, feeds tuned to each person and, more and more, a conversation with an AI tool. No single channel reaches everyone anymore.

For B2B, that shift is even sharper. Buyers have never been more prepared. By the time they call you, they've already done most of their research on their own.

## The tension: reach versus relevance

On the one hand, broad reach still feels like progress. Big impression numbers look great in a board deck. A splashy campaign gets people talking internally.

But reach without relevance is mostly waste. The person who sees your ad between two other things they didn't ask for is not shopping. The person typing "[your category] for [their industry]" into a search bar is.

Where I land: put relevance first. Reach is something you add once the moments that decide deals are covered.

## Where buyers actually go now

When a B2B buyer has a problem, they usually:

- **Search.** They type the problem, the category or a competitor's name plus "alternative."
- **Ask AI.** They ask ChatGPT or another tool who they should consider, and they get three or four names back.
- **Ask peers.** They post in a community or message someone they trust.
- **Read comparisons.** Reviews, side-by-side pages and pricing breakdowns.

Notice what these have in common. The buyer is pulling information, not waiting for it to be pushed at them. Your job is to be there when they pull.

## Why bottom-of-funnel pages fill the gap

The searches buyers make right before choosing are small compared with broad category terms. That used to make them look unimportant. Now they're where the decisions happen.

A page that honestly compares you with the tool a buyer is using today, or explains what something costs and why, gets far less traffic than a general guide. But the people who read it are close to a decision. And those same pages are the kind of clear, specific answers AI tools pull from when they recommend vendors.

So if mass media is the channel you're losing, bottom-of-funnel content is the channel you can own.

## A worked example

Picture two companies with the same quarterly budget.

The first spends most of it on broad awareness: a sponsorship, a display campaign and a general brand video. The report shows big reach. Sales asks where the leads are.

The second spends it building pages for the exact searches buyers make before choosing: comparisons with the top three competitors, an alternatives page, a pricing explainer and a guide for each of its two best-fit industries. Traffic is modest. But the people who show up are shopping, and the sales team starts hearing "I found you when I was comparing options."

Neither approach is free of risk. Only one is aimed at people who are ready to buy.

## What still deserves some broad reach

None of this means brand doesn't matter. The silent mass of people who hear your name and remember it later is real, even if you can't attribute it.

The difference is order. Cover the moments of decision first, then add reach in the specific places your niche already gathers: the one podcast they all listen to, the community where they ask each other for advice, the newsletter they actually open. That's still reach. It's just reach aimed at people who look like your buyers, instead of everyone at once.

## Common mistakes

- **Chasing the old channel in a new costume.** Buying broad reach on a new platform is still buying broad reach.
- **Ignoring AI answers.** If buyers ask an AI tool for a shortlist and you're not on it, you never get the chance to compete.
- **Measuring impressions.** They always go up with more spend. Measure qualified conversations instead.

## What to do this week

Write down the five questions your best customers asked right before they bought. Search each one on Google, and ask each one in ChatGPT. Note who shows up. If it isn't you, that's the gap mass media was never going to close.
