If your marketing isn't generating pipeline, the usual reason is that the activity isn't connected to anything: no clear buyer, no clear problem, and nothing answering buyers at the moment they're ready to choose. Fix the foundation and the bottom of the funnel before you add more activity.

Two people I talked with recently described the same thing from different angles. One said what he normally sees is activity that's disconnected. Let's go to this conference, let's sponsor this, let's push out some LinkedIn ads. The other said she keeps stepping into teams doing random acts of marketing, with no direction and no strategy.

Neither of them was blaming the teams. Busy marketing teams usually mean well. They just don't have a foundation to work from.

## Why random acts happen

It's easy to understand how a team ends up here. A board member suggests a conference. Sales asks for a one-pager. A competitor launches a webinar series, so you need one too. Each request is reasonable. Nobody steps back to ask how they fit together.

The result looks productive. Lots of posts, lots of events, a full calendar. And pipeline doesn't move.

## Start with the commercial foundation

Before any activity, you need clear answers to three questions:

- **Who buys?** The kind of company and the person who signs.
- **What problem are they solving?** In their words, not yours.
- **What do they do right before they choose a vendor?** What they search, what they ask an AI tool, what they compare.

That last one is the one most teams skip. And it's where the money is.

## Fund the bottom first

Most marketing budgets are upside down. They spend heavily on awareness, the top of the funnel, and almost nothing on the moment a buyer is deciding.

But the buyers typing "[competitor] alternative" or "[category] pricing" into Google already have a problem and a budget. When they ask ChatGPT who to use, they get three or four names. If you're not one of them, all the awareness in the world won't save that deal.

So start at the bottom:

- List the searches buyers make right before they choose.
- Build a page that answers each one truthfully and well.
- Make each page convert that specific reader.

Then add top-of-funnel activity once the bottom is catching what it sends.

## Run every activity through a simple filter

Before committing budget or time to anything, ask:

- Which buyer stage is this for?
- Which problem does it speak to?
- How will we know if it produced pipeline?

If you can't answer all three, pause it. That's not saying no forever. It's saying not yet.

## Measure what matters

Random acts survive because they're measured on activity. Posts published. Booth visitors. Impressions. Those numbers always go up when you do more.

Measure qualified leads and pipeline instead. It's a harder number to move, and it tells you the truth about what's working.

## The payoff

When activity is connected to a foundation, everything gets easier. The message is consistent, so buyers recognize you. The budget goes further, because it isn't spread across ten unrelated bets. And when something works, you can tell why, so you can do more of it.

Traffic is vanity. Pipeline is sanity.

## Signs you're doing random acts of marketing

- You can't say which buyer stage last quarter's biggest campaign was for.
- Events get booked because a competitor is going.
- The marketing report is all activity: posts, impressions, booth visitors.

## A worked example

Say a company has budget for a mid-size conference booth this quarter. Run it through the filter. Which buyer stage? Mostly awareness. Which problem? Unclear. How will we measure pipeline? Badge scans. Now compare it with building five pages for the comparison and pricing searches buyers make right before they choose. The second option is less exciting and much easier to connect to pipeline. That doesn't mean never do the conference. It means do the bottom of the funnel first.

## What to do this week

List every marketing activity planned for the next 90 days. Next to each, write the buyer stage and how you'll measure pipeline. Pause anything you can't fill in.

## Common mistakes

- **Copying competitors' calendars.** Their event list isn't your strategy.
- **Measuring activity.** More posts always looks like progress, even when pipeline is flat.
