How to get your B2B company cited by ChatGPT

How to get cited by ChatGPT, for a B2B company, is a source problem. The model cites third-party listicles, directories, Reddit threads, and your own pages that answer a buying question in the opening sentences. Structured data and an llms.txt file help an agent read those pages, and neither one earns the citation on its own.

What does ChatGPT pull a citation from?

When a buyer asks which vendors to shortlist, ChatGPT retrieves a handful of URLs and names some of them. For a B2B buying question, the same kinds of sources come back. Five of them decide whether your company is in the answer.

  1. Third-party listicles and directories. Roundups and review categories already compare options, so they compress into a shortlist. Miss those lists and you are absent even when your site ranks for your name.
  2. Reddit and other public threads. Buyers ask who they use, and those threads get retrieved often, including for this question. You do not control the replies. You do control whether a clear page exists beside them.
  3. Your own answer pages. A page on your domain is cited when it answers one buying question in the buyer's words and that page is retrieved. A homepage slogan almost never is.
  4. Structured data. Organization, WebPage, FAQPage and BreadcrumbList markup label who published the page and which question it answers. The sentences are what gets quoted.
  5. llms.txt. A plain-text file at the site root listing the canonical pages and what each one is for. Agents that fetch it get a map. A map of only awareness posts hands over only awareness posts.

A citation is a retrieved page plus a sentence worth lifting. More pages help only when they are the kind of source the model is already looking for.

Why do listicles, directories and Reddit get cited before your blog?

Because the buyer's question is comparative, and those sources already are. A listicle that says who each vendor is for can be turned into a shortlist. A Reddit thread is practitioners naming who they hired and who they left. A tactic post, or a definition of the category, answers a different question than who to hire.

You cannot force a directory to add you, and you cannot script the replies in a thread. Describe the company the same way on the site, in listings, and in the sentence other people repeat, so a mention and your page agree. Publish the comparison, alternative and use-case pages those roundups summarize, so the model has a primary source beside the list. Treat those third-party pages as part of the work, not as something that happens after the blog calendar is full.

When the site, the listings and the profiles describe you differently, the model blends them into a vague sentence. We wrote that up as why ChatGPT can't describe your company.

What does a page on your site need in order to be cited?

It has to be the answer, not an introduction to the answer. The first paragraph under the heading should respond in two or three sentences a model can lift without the rest of the page. The heading should be the question. One page, one decision.

A commercial page that gets cited says who the offer is for and who it is a poor fit for, states the trade-off, and matches a query a buyer asks while choosing: an alternative, a versus, a pricing-intent question, or a use case a category page cannot answer. It uses one description of the company everywhere, and it links to the pages that support the claim. A definition built for a head term can collect searches and still be useless when someone asks which product fits a specific team. A paragraph any vendor could publish has nothing to quote. That bar is the same one in content buyers still trust.

Where do structured data and llms.txt fit?

They make a page legible. They do not make it worth citing.

On a page like this, structured data is JSON-LD: a WebPage naming the publisher, an FAQPage whose questions match the body, and a BreadcrumbList. It stops a parser from guessing which paragraph is the answer. A vague answer stays vague in the markup too.

llms.txt sits at /llms.txt. Ours lists the public pages and a one-line description of each, then the notes in the resources library. Write yours as a map of the pages you want an agent to read, in the same words as those pages. Update it when a money page ships. A file that points at an old blog, or at pages that contradict each other, is a bad map.

A model does not have to fetch llms.txt, and schema does not beat a listicle that already answers the question. Both belong on a citeable site. Neither replaces the page, or the third-party source that mentioned you first.

Why do money pages get cited when traffic posts do not?

A money page is the question a buyer asks while building a shortlist. It is a comparison, an alternatives page, a pricing-intent page, or a use-case page. The query is narrow, so a specific answer can be retrieved even when you will not outrank a category leader on a head term. A traffic post defines a term and lists tips. Answer engines already produce that summary, and when they cite it they cite a large definition, not another tip list.

Three things have to be true together. The page answers a query a buyer asks while choosing. The answer is specific enough to lift: who it is for, the trade-off, the neighboring options. And something off your domain agrees, a listicle, a directory, or a thread, so the model is not asked to trust a vendor page alone.

An AEO program with no pages, and an SEO program that only publishes top-of-funnel posts, both miss that. One measures mentions of pages that do not exist. The other produces pages for questions a model answers on its own. Ranking and getting cited are two different jobs, and we treat them that way.

Questions about getting cited.

How do I get my B2B company cited by ChatGPT?

Publish pages that answer one buying question in the first paragraph, keep the company description identical everywhere you appear, and show up in the third-party listicles, directories and threads ChatGPT already retrieves. Schema and an llms.txt file make those pages readable. They do not replace them.

Does FAQ schema get you cited by ChatGPT?

No. FAQ schema repeats a question and an answer that are already visible on the page. It helps a parser. A citation still depends on the page being retrieved and on the answer being specific enough to quote.

Can we get cited without a blog?

Yes. The pages that get cited on buying questions are usually comparisons, alternatives, pricing-intent pages and use-case pages, plus the roundups and directories that mention you. A blog that only defines terms is the part you can skip.

Why does ChatGPT cite Reddit and not our site?

Because the thread answers who people use, with named options, and a lot of sites answer with a slogan. A page that states who you are for, the trade-off, and the neighboring choices gives the model a primary source to put next to the thread.

How long does it take to get cited?

There is no clock you can set. Narrow buying queries, where the current answer is a thin thread or an outdated list, move sooner than broad category questions owned by a few large sites. On the 15-minute call we tell you which of yours is which.
